## English version (shortened)

Achieving high-consistency frequency-ratio measurements between different-species optical clocks at different institutions is a core requirement for redefining the SI second. Many clocks now reach self-evaluated uncertainties better than 2 × 10⁻¹⁸, yet frequency ratios with uncertainty better than 5 × 10⁻¹⁸ remain rare: only a 2026 NIST–JILA ytterbium/strontium ratio has met this requirement, and it differs substantially from earlier reports. Here we link an IAPMST ytterbium clock (Wuhan) to a USTC strontium clock (Shanghai) about 700 km away, by fibre, and measure the Yb/Sr ratio. The site geopotential difference, ΔW = −280.05 m²/s², is obtained by spirit levelling. From 58 days and 280 hours of data, after correcting the tidal gravitational redshift and applying a Bayesian treatment, we obtain Yb/Sr = 1.207 507 039 343 337 7215(23), deviating from the latest NIST–JILA value 1.207 507 039 343 337 7230(37) by −1.5 × 10⁻¹⁸ and from the European value by +3.5 × 10⁻¹⁸. The data resolve the tidal gravitational-redshift modulation at 6.4σ significance; correcting it lowers the reduced chi-square from 5.42 to 3.70 (32%). Such an inter-city, cross-species clock network both supports redefinition and senses the time-varying gravitational potential, resolving its difference at the millimetre level.

**Keywords:** optical lattice clock; Yb/Sr frequency ratio; fibre-link clock
comparison; SI second redefinition; gravitational redshift; tidal potential
