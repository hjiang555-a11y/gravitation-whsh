## English version (shortened)

High-consistency frequency-ratio measurements between different-species optical clocks at different institutions are essential for redefining the SI second. While many clocks now achieve self-evaluated uncertainties below 2 × 10⁻¹⁸, only one Yb/Sr ratio—the 2026 NIST–JILA result—reaches the required uncertainty below 5 × 10⁻¹⁸, yet it disagrees significantly with earlier values. Here we link the IAPMST ytterbium clock (Wuhan) and the USTC strontium clock (Shanghai), about 700 km apart, via a fibre network and measure their Yb/Sr ratio. The geopotential difference, ΔW = 280.05 m²/s², is determined by spirit levelling. From 58 days (280 hours) of data, after correcting for the tidal gravitational redshift and applying a Bayesian analysis, we obtain a Bayesian statistical uncertainty of `0.75 × 10⁻¹⁸` (clock statistical noise only, excluding clock evaluation), giving Yb/Sr = 1.207 507 039 343 337 7215(23)—1.5 × 10⁻¹⁸ below the latest NIST–JILA value and 3.5 × 10⁻¹⁸ above the latest European value. The tidal-redshift modulation is resolved at 6.4σ, and its correction reduces the reduced χ² from 5.42 to 3.70 (32%). This inter-city, cross-species clock network thus supports both the redefinition of the SI second and millimetre-level sensing of the time-varying gravitational potential.

**Keywords:** optical lattice clock; Yb/Sr frequency ratio; fibre-link clock
comparison; SI second redefinition; gravitational redshift; tidal potential
