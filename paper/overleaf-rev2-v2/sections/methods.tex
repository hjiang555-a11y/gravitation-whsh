\subsection*{Leveling determination of the geopotential difference}

The static gravitational-redshift correction is fixed by the
geopotential difference between the atomic-transition positions of the
two clocks, determined by a first-order spirit leveling campaign
carried out specifically for this comparison: a
$\levelKm$\,km line between the Wuhan benchmark WS-03 and the
Shanghai benchmark WS-01, observed in both directions with digital
levels and bar-code invar staffs, with gravity observed along the
line. The geopotential difference is integrated section by section,
$\dW_{AB} = -\sum_i g_i\,\Delta H_i$, and combined with the local
benchmark-to-transition offsets, giving
$\dW = \dWVal \pm \dWUnc~\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$, equivalent to an
optical fractional shift of $\deltaGV$. The per-kilometer accidental
error $M_\Delta = \levelMm$\,mm\,km$^{-1}$ satisfies the most
stringent first-order requirements of the Chinese, US, German and
Czech national standards (Supplementary
Section~3)~\citep{fgcc1984,gb12897,cuzk2015,advfapn2022}. An
independent GNSS-plus-EGM2008 geoid-model calibration gives
$\dWAlt \pm \dWAltUnc~\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$, consistent with the
leveled value but about 30 times less precise, so it serves as a
consistency check rather than a calibration at the reported
accuracy; a meaningful independent verification will require
transportable optical clocks with higher frequency reproducibility.

\subsection*{Experimental setup and frequency transfer}

The two sites are connected by a phase-stabilized fiber link of
$\fibreKm$\,km single-pass length ($\roundKm$\,km loop-back),
operated at \qty{1550}{nm} and carried on a commercial telecom
network used non-exclusively. The route is divided into spans of tens
of kilometers, each terminated by a relay station carrying a
narrow-linewidth laser, an erbium-doped fiber amplifier and a
fiber-noise-cancellation loop; the added instability of the link is
kept below the clock noise for averaging times beyond $100$\,s.

Both ends use independent optical frequency combs, self-referenced
and phase-locked to their local clock, to bridge the spectral gap
between the clock transitions and the telecom carrier; all frequencies
in the chain derive from the two clock transitions through fixed
factor-of-two relations, $f_{\mathrm{comb}}^{1397} =
\tfrac{1}{2}(1+a)\,f_{\mathrm{Sr}}$ and $f_{\mathrm{comb}}^{1156} =
\tfrac{1}{2}(1+b)\,f_{\mathrm{Yb}}$, where $a$ and $b$ collect the Sr
and Yb systematic corrections (density, lattice, second-order Zeeman
and blackbody shifts). The observable is the
out-of-loop beat between the received carrier and the local
Yb-referenced comb, converted to fractional units with the transfer
frequency $f_{1550} = \fFifteenTHz$\,THz. The static
gravitational-redshift correction enters the ratio inversion as a
multiplicative factor,
$\left(\mathrm{Yb/Sr}\right)_{\rm corr}
= \left(\mathrm{Yb/Sr}\right)_{\rm raw} \times (1+\delta_g)$ with
$\delta_g = \deltaGV$, derived from the leveled geopotential
difference above.

\subsection*{Data processing and segment definition}

The counters sample the beat every second in phase-averaging mode.
Each accepted segment contributes its longest jump-free record.
Supplementary Table~1 lists the per-segment retained counts, ratio
deviations and statistical uncertainties; five segments whose
stability slopes fall outside the white-frequency-noise band are
flagged there as model-limited. These five lie at the steep end of a
continuous slope distribution, just beyond the acceptance bound, and
show no common campaign, weather or laboratory condition that
distinguishes them from the remaining segments.

As an additional stability check, the \nSeg~segments were spliced
end-to-end on a continuous 1-s clock with the wall-clock gaps
removed, giving a single \nSamples~sample record whose overlapping
Allan deviation $\sigma_y(\tau)$ can reach averaging times beyond a
single segment (Figure~\ref{fig:spliced}). The spliced curve extends
the per-segment stability points to $\tau$ up to about 4.6 days of
pure data time, at which the raw record reaches
$\sigma_y \approx 8.6\times10^{-19}$; applying the two theoretical
tide corrections ($A = +1$) shifts the long-$\tau$ level by less than
$10\%$ in opposite senses, and the removed correction terms
($h/f_{1550}$) themselves lie below the corrected curves. Because the
spliced axis removes the inter-segment gaps, points with $\tau$
exceeding one segment mix different campaigns; the pair count behind
each point is reported with the data. This diagnostic is a data
record, not a statistical uncertainty.

\subsection*{Clock-ratio determination, statistical model and uncertainty budget}

Each retained segment yields a clock-ratio estimate $R_i$ from the mean
of its retained beat samples, inverted through the fixed frequency-chain
relations and corrected for the static gravitational redshift
$\delta_g = \deltaGV$ derived from the leveled geopotential difference
(see above). The per-segment statistical uncertainty $u_i$ is obtained
from the overlapping Allan deviation of the beat fractional frequency,
evaluated on a tau grid of powers of two plus 600, 1200, 3600 and
7200\,s, all limited to a quarter of the longest gap-free run
$T_i$ of the segment: $u_i$ adopts the last computable point
$u_i = R_i\,\sigma_y(\tau_{\rm max})$ without extrapolating the
stability curve beyond the data. The log-log slope of $\sigma_y$
against $\tau$ is computed as a diagnostic; segments whose slope
leaves the white-frequency-noise band are flagged but retained.

The segment estimates are then combined under a random-effect model
with a common excess scatter~\citep{birge1932errors,
mandel1970interlaboratory}: each
segment is modeled as $R_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, u_i^2 + \xi^2)$,
with $\xi$ added in quadrature to the
per-segment uncertainties. The primary value is obtained from a
Bayesian random-effect model, in which $\mu$ and $\xi$ are
jointly inferred with flat priors on $\mu$ and on $\log\xi$, and the
joint posterior $p(\mu, \xi \mid \{R_i, u_i\})$ is marginalized
numerically over a 40-decade $\log\xi$ grid, with the grid span
expanded until the posterior mass at the edges falls below 1\%; the
posterior mean of
$\mu$ and its posterior standard deviation give the reported central
value and statistical uncertainty, with
$\xi = \xiBayesE \times 10^{-18}$. As cross-checks, the same
observation model is combined by weighted least squares
(inverse-variance weights $w_i = 1/u_i^2$) and by the Mandel--Paule
estimator~\citep{mandel1970interlaboratory}; the resulting
$\chi^2_{\rm red}$ and $\xi$ values are given in
Table~\ref{tab:estimators}.

The remaining terms of the uncertainty budget (Table~\ref{tab:budget})
are systematic. The Sr and Yb terms are the published evaluations of
the two clocks~\citep{jia2026sr, zhu2026yb}; the value adopted for the
Yb clock is the published evaluation of
$\uYbE\times10^{-18}$~\citep{zhu2026yb}, which is more conservative
than an internal estimate, and the discrepancy does not affect the
conclusion at the reported precision. The static-potential term is
$u(\dW)/c^2$ from the leveling uncertainty
$\dWUnc~\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$, giving
$\uStaticE \times 10^{-18}$. The frequency-conversion stages
contribute below $\uSubsystemBound$ each, from the digitization error
of the direct-digital-synthesis frequency-reference settings; the
budget carries them as \qty{0.1e-18} upper bounds.

\subsection*{Tidal template and time-varying redshift}

The time-varying gravitational-redshift term is quantified from a
tidal template: the solid Earth tide and ocean tide loading
displacement difference between the two sites, computed every
\qty{30}{s} with the public software ETideLoad4.5, converted to an
equivalent geopotential difference and then to a beat-frequency
template $h = f_{1550}\,\Delta W/c^2$ with the same $f_{1550}$
normalization as the beat record. A second, independent template is
built from the DE421 ephemerides and the FES2022 ocean-tide model on
a \qty{90}{s} grid.

To test how well the templates fit the data, we repeated the ratio
combination with the tide series applied before the inversion, at two
fixed response amplitudes: the theoretical amplitude $A = +1$ and the
historical amplitude $A = +0.54$ from the demeaned beat waveform of
our earlier analysis of the same campaign. Both templates degrade
rather than improve the fit: the reduced chi-square of the
combination rises from \tidalChiRaw~to \tidalChiTheory~and
\tidalChiEmp~for the ETideLoad4.5 template (Bayesian center shift
$-\tidalShiftEmpE\times10^{-18}$), and to \tidalChiAlt~for the
DE421+FES2022 template, which moves the center in the opposite
direction, by $+\tidalShiftAltE\times10^{-18}$. Repeating the
segment-mean correlation with the solid tide alone
($r = \tidalCorrSolid$ instead of \tidalCorrTotal) locates the
dominant coupling in the ocean-tide loading term.

Templates that degrade the segment agreement, and whose two
independent realizations disagree in sign, are treated as
inconsistent with the data: no tidal correction is applied to the
reported central value, and the larger of the two center shifts is
carried as the time-varying-redshift uncertainty item
$\uTidalE\times10^{-18}$.

\subsection*{Within-segment tidal correlation analysis}
The within-segment correlation analysis uses the
same 1200-s triangular window as the ratio processing: the retained
beat record of each segment and the 1-s tidal template are both
window-averaged with a 1200-s triangular window at 600-s stride,
demeaned, and fitted through the origin as
$\mathrm{beat} = A\cdot\mathrm{tide}$, with the template sign
corrected for the beat polarity so that a positive amplitude
corresponds to the expected response $A = +1$; the amplitude, the
Pearson correlation $r$ and its $p$ value are inferred from the
non-overlapping 1200-s windows only, with the per-segment amplitude
uncertainty obtained from the scatter of the non-overlapping
residuals. Because the two models are correlated realizations that
share the same beat record, their two significance values are not
independent replications of the measurement. The combined response
amplitudes are $\tidalAmpOldE$ (ETideLoad4.5) and $\tidalAmpNewE$
(DE421+FES2022), as reported in the main text.

The components are treated as independent
and combined in quadrature, giving the total standard uncertainty
$\uTotalE \times 10^{-18}$.
