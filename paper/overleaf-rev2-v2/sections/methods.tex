\subsection*{Leveling determination of the geopotential difference}

The static gravitational-redshift correction is fixed by the
geopotential difference between the atomic-transition positions of the
two clocks, determined by a first-order spirit leveling campaign
carried out specifically for this comparison. The leveling line, about
$\levelKm$\,km long, runs between the Wuhan benchmark WS-03 (at
IAPMST) and the Shanghai benchmark WS-01 (at USTC) through Ezhou,
Huangshi, Huanggang, Lu'an, Hefei, Wuhu, Guangde and Suzhou. Digital
levels were used with bar-code invar staffs, with sight lengths not
exceeding \qty{30}{m}; the line was observed in \qtyrange{6}{8}{km}
sections in both directions, each checked in the field. Gravity was
observed along the line at the leveling points with two relative
gravimeters. The orthometric height difference between the benchmarks
follows from the leveled normal heights after the
normal-to-orthometric conversion, and the geopotential difference is
integrated section by section, $\dW_{AB} = -\sum_i g_i\,\Delta H_i$,
where $g_i$ is the mean gravity and $\Delta H_i$ the orthometric
height difference of the $i$-th section. Combining this with the two
local offsets between benchmark and clock platform (obtained by
trigonometric leveling) and the platform-to-transition offsets from
the clock design parameters gives the atomic-transition geopotential
difference $\dW = \dWVal \pm \dWUnc~\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$, equivalent
to an optical fractional shift of $\deltaGV$. The accidental error
per kilometer of the leveling is
$M_\Delta = \levelMm$\,mm\,km$^{-1}$, which satisfies the most
stringent requirements for first-order leveling in the national
standards of China, the United States, Germany and
Czechia~\citep{fgcc1984,gb12897,cuzk2015,advfapn2022}.

As an independent check, the geopotential difference was also
determined by a combined GNSS and geoid-model calibration: the
ellipsoidal heights of the permanent marks were measured by precise
point positioning, their vertical offsets to the clock reference
surfaces were leveled locally, and the EGM2008 geoid model was used
to convert to a geopotential difference of
$\dWAlt \pm \dWAltUnc~\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$. The two independent
determinations agree within the uncertainties of the GNSS-based
value, but the GNSS-plus-geoid calibration is two orders of magnitude
less precise than the leveling result, so it cannot act as an
effective cross-check at the reported accuracy; a meaningful
independent verification will require transportable optical clocks
with higher frequency reproducibility.

\subsection*{Experimental setup and frequency transfer}

The comparison links two optical lattice clocks at different institutions:
an ytterbium (Yb) clock at IAPMST in Wuhan and a strontium (Sr) clock at
USTC in Shanghai, about $\siteKm$\,km apart. The two sites are connected by
a phase-stabilized fiber link of $\fibreKm$\,km single-pass length
($\roundKm$\,km loop-back), operated at \qty{1550}{nm}. The route is
divided into spans of tens of kilometers, each terminated by a relay
station that carries a narrow-linewidth laser together with an
erbium-doped fiber amplifier and a fiber-noise-cancellation loop; the
added instability of the link is kept below the clock noise level for
averaging times beyond $100$\,s.

Both ends use independent optical frequency combs to bridge the
spectral gap between the clock transitions and the telecom carrier. On the
Shanghai side the Sr \qty{698}{nm} transition is transferred via a
\qty{1397}{nm} continuous laser to the \qty{1550}{nm} carrier; on the
Wuhan side the received carrier is linked to the Yb \qty{578}{nm}
transition through the \qty{1156}{nm} light. All local frequency
references and phase-locked loops are derived from the comb repetition
rate $f_{\mathrm{rep}} = \fRepMHz$\,MHz. The observable is the
out-of-loop beat between the received carrier and the local
Yb-referenced comb, converted to fractional units with the transfer
frequency $f_{1550} = \fFifteenTHz$\,THz. All frequencies in the chain
derive from the two clock transitions through fixed factor-of-two
relations,
$f_{\mathrm{comb}}^{1397} = \tfrac{1}{2}(1+a)\,f_{\mathrm{Sr}}$ and
$f_{\mathrm{comb}}^{1156} = \tfrac{1}{2}(1+b)\,f_{\mathrm{Yb}}$, where
$a$ and $b$ collect the Sr and Yb systematic corrections (density,
lattice, second-order Zeeman and blackbody shifts). The static
gravitational-redshift correction enters the ratio inversion as a
multiplicative factor,
$\left(\mathrm{Yb/Sr}\right)_{\rm corr}
= \left(\mathrm{Yb/Sr}\right)_{\rm raw} \times (1+\delta_g)$ with
$\delta_g = \deltaGV$, derived from the leveled geopotential
difference above.

\subsection*{Clock-ratio determination, statistical model and uncertainty budget}

Each retained segment yields a clock-ratio estimate $R_i$ from the mean
of its retained beat samples, inverted through the fixed frequency-chain
relations and corrected for the static gravitational redshift
$\delta_g = \deltaGV$ derived from the leveled geopotential difference
(see above). The per-segment statistical uncertainty $u_i$ is obtained
from the overlapping Allan deviation of the beat fractional frequency,
evaluated on a tau grid of powers of two plus 600, 1200, 3600 and
7200\,s, all limited to a quarter of the longest gap-free run
$T_i$ of the segment: $u_i$ adopts the last computable point
$u_i = R_i\,\sigma_y(\tau_{\rm max})$ without extrapolating the
stability curve beyond the data. The log-log slope of $\sigma_y$
against $\tau$ is computed as a diagnostic; segments whose slope
leaves the white-frequency-noise band are flagged but retained.

The segment estimates are then combined under a random-effect model
with a common excess scatter~\citep{birge1932errors,
mandel1970interlaboratory}: each
segment is modeled as $R_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, u_i^2 + \xi^2)$,
with $\xi$ added in quadrature to the
per-segment uncertainties. The primary value is obtained from a
Bayesian random-effect model, in which $\mu$ and $\xi$ are
jointly inferred with flat priors on $\mu$ and on $\log\xi$, and the
joint posterior $p(\mu, \xi \mid \{R_i, u_i\})$ is marginalized
numerically over a 40-decade $\log\xi$ grid, with the grid span
expanded until the posterior mass at the edges falls below 1\%; the
posterior mean of
$\mu$ and its posterior standard deviation give the reported central
value and statistical uncertainty, with
$\xi = \xiBayesE \times 10^{-18}$. As cross-checks, the same
observation model is combined by weighted least squares
(inverse-variance weights $w_i = 1/u_i^2$) and by the Mandel--Paule
estimator~\citep{mandel1970interlaboratory}; the resulting
$\chi^2_{\rm red}$ and $\xi$ values are given in
Table~\ref{tab:estimators}.

The remaining terms of the uncertainty budget (Table~\ref{tab:budget})
are systematic. The Sr and Yb terms are the published evaluations of
the two clocks~\citep{jia2026sr, zhu2026yb}; the value adopted for the
Yb clock resolves an internal-versus-published discrepancy in favor
of the published evaluation. The static-potential term is
$u(\dW)/c^2$ from the leveling uncertainty
$\dWUnc~\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$, giving
$\uStaticE \times 10^{-18}$. The frequency-conversion stages
contribute $\uLinkE \times 10^{-18}$ and $\uCombE \times 10^{-18}$
from their frequency-reference settings.

\subsection*{Tidal template and time-varying redshift}

The time-varying gravitational-redshift term is quantified from a
tidal template. The supplied tide series consists of the solid Earth
tide and ocean tide loading displacement difference between the two
sites, computed every \qty{30}{s} with the public software
ETideLoad4.5~\citep{zhang2026etideload}, which implements the
geophysical models and numerical standards of the IERS Conventions
(2010)~\citep{iers2010conventions}. The displacement difference is
converted to an equivalent geopotential difference with the local
gravity $g$ and then to a beat-frequency template through
$h = f_{1550}\,\Delta W/c^2$, the same normalization used for the
beat record.

To test how well the template fits the data, we repeated the ratio
combination with the tide series applied before the inversion, at two
fixed response amplitudes: the theoretical amplitude $A = +1$ and the
historical amplitude $A = -0.54$ obtained from the demeaned beat
waveform in our earlier analysis of the same campaign. The fit
degrades rather than improves: the reduced chi-square of the
combination rises from $\tidalChiRaw$ to $\tidalChiTheory$ and
$\tidalChiEmp$, respectively, and the Bayesian center moves by
$\tidalShiftEmpE\times10^{-18}$. A second, independent tide template
was evaluated as a cross-check: the solid tide was computed from the
DE421 ephemerides (Earth--Sun--Moon positions) with Love numbers, and
the ocean-tide loading correction to the gravitational potential was
taken directly from the FES2022 global ocean-tide model, on a
\qty{90}{s} Beijing-time grid. Its fixed-amplitude combination again
degrades the fit ($\chi^2_{\rm red} = \tidalChiAlt$ at the historical
amplitude) but moves the Bayesian center in the opposite direction,
by $\tidalShiftAltE\times10^{-18}$. To separate the solid-tide and
ocean-loading contributions, we also repeated the segment-mean
correlation with the solid tide alone: the Pearson correlation of the
ratio deviations with the segment-mean full tide is
$\tidalCorrTotal$, which falls to $\tidalCorrSolid$ for the solid
tide only, indicating that the dominant coupling comes from the
ocean-tide loading term.

Templates that degrade the segment agreement, and whose two
independent realizations disagree in sign, are treated as
inconsistent with the data: no tidal correction is applied to the
reported central value, and the larger of the two center shifts is
carried as the time-varying-redshift uncertainty item
$\uTidalE\times10^{-18}$.

\subsection*{Within-segment tidal correlation analysis}

The within-segment correlation reported in the Discussion uses the
same 1200-s triangular window as the ratio processing: the retained
beat record of each segment and the 1-s tidal template are both
window-averaged with a 1200-s triangular window at 600-s stride,
demeaned, and fitted through the origin as
$\mathrm{beat} = A\cdot\mathrm{tide}$, with the template sign
corrected for the beat polarity so that a positive amplitude
corresponds to the expected response $A = +1$; the amplitude, the
Pearson correlation $r$ and its $p$ value are inferred from the
non-overlapping 1200-s windows only, with the per-segment amplitude
uncertainty obtained from the scatter of the non-overlapping
residuals. Figure~\ref{fig:tidal-response}a shows this fit for one
segment: the template varies at the $10^{-17}$ level while the
single-segment beat residuals are comparable in size, so no single
segment reaches significance on its own. The same fit is repeated for
both tide models, on the same \nSeg~segments that enter the ratio
combination (Figure~\ref{fig:tidal-response}b). The per-segment
amplitudes are combined with precision weights $1/u_A^2$; the sign
consistency is evaluated by a two-sided binomial test on the number of
segments with positive correlation, together with a signed Stouffer
combination of the per-segment $p$ values. The combined correlation is
significant for both models, but neither model reproduces the expected
response amplitude $+1$ (the observed precision-weighted amplitudes
are $\tidalAmpOldE$ and $\tidalAmpNewE$), and the two models disagree
with each other, so the observed coupling is smaller than the
templates predict and the tidal term is kept as an uncertainty rather
than a correction, as in the ratio combination above.
