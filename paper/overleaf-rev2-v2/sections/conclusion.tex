We have compared an ytterbium lattice clock in Wuhan and a strontium
lattice clock in Shanghai over a \fibreKm\,km fiber link and
determined the Yb/Sr frequency ratio with a total standard
uncertainty of \uTotalE$\times10^{-18}$. Together with the NIST--JILA
measurement, this provides, for the first time, two independent
determinations of the Yb/Sr ratio in agreement at the precision
required by the roadmap for the redefinition of the SI second,
confirming one of the required ratio measurements. The two sites now
form a working node pair that lays the foundation for extending the
network further: joining more optical-clock nodes through terrestrial
fiber backbones and interconnecting them across continents by
satellite and free-space links will turn the comparison demonstrated
here into a global optical-clock network. This measurement is a
milestone on the necessary path toward that network: it will provide
the verifiable metrological infrastructure that the redefinition of
the SI second requires and, beyond it, will support chronometric
leveling of the gravitational potential at centimeter resolution and
distributed searches for new physics with clock arrays.
