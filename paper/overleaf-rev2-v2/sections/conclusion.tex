We have compared an ytterbium lattice clock in Wuhan and a strontium
lattice clock in Shanghai over a \fibreKm\,km fiber link and
determined the Yb/Sr frequency ratio with a total standard
uncertainty of \uTotalE$\times10^{-18}$. This is, to our
knowledge, the first intercity, cross-species ratio measurement to
meet the uncertainty requirement of the roadmap for the redefinition
of the SI second, and it brings the three independent Yb/Sr
determinations into agreement at the few-$10^{-18}$ level. The
remaining limitations are the systematic evaluations of the two
clocks and the geodetic treatment of the gravitational potential,
whose time-varying component is not yet correctable with available
tide models. With these addressed, the two sites form a working node
pair that lays the foundation for extending the network further:
joining more optical-clock nodes through terrestrial fiber backbones
and interconnecting them across continents by satellite and
free-space links will turn the comparison demonstrated here into a
global optical-clock network. This measurement is a milestone on the
necessary path toward that network: it will provide the
verifiable metrological infrastructure that the redefinition of the
SI second requires and, beyond it, will support chronometric leveling
of the gravitational potential at centimeter resolution and
distributed searches for new physics with clock arrays.
