Redefining the International System of Units (SI) second requires
independent frequency-ratio measurements between clocks based on
different atomic transitions in different laboratories, with an
overall uncertainty below \refThreshold{} and consistent results. However, the
self-assessed uncertainties of these clocks have not yet been validated
by such comparisons. We compared a ytterbium optical lattice clock in
Wuhan with a strontium optical lattice clock in Shanghai---about
$\siteKm$\,km apart---over a phase-stabilized fiber link spanning
$\fibreKm$\,km, and determine the Yb/Sr frequency ratio to be
\Rheadline(\RheadlineUnc), with a Bayesian statistical uncertainty of
\uStatShort$\times10^{-18}$ and a total uncertainty of
\uTotalE$\times10^{-18}$. The value differs from the NIST--JILA
result~\refNistV(\refNistU) by \dVsNistE{}, consistent within the
combined standard uncertainty. To our knowledge, this is the first
intercity, cross-species frequency-ratio measurement to meet the
uncertainty requirement of the roadmap for the redefinition of the SI
second, and together with the NIST--JILA result it provides the
second, independent determination of the Yb/Sr ratio by a different
institute, as required by criterion I.2.b for this pair of
transitions. The measurement also demonstrates, at intercity scale,
the capability that a wide-area optical-clock network requires.
