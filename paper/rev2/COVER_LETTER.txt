Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Intercity Yb/Sr
optical clock comparison over 1 350 km fiber link at 2.3×10⁻¹⁸" for
consideration as a Letter in Physical Review Letters.

Our work reports the first intercity, cross-species frequency-ratio
measurement to meet the uncertainty requirement of criterion I.2.b in
the roadmap for the redefinition of the SI second: an ytterbium
lattice clock at IAPMST in Wuhan and a strontium lattice clock at
USTC in Shanghai, about 700 km apart, compared over a phase-stabilized
1 350 km fiber link on a non-exclusive commercial telecom network. We
determine the Yb/Sr frequency ratio to be 1.207 507 039 343 337 720
1(28), with a total standard uncertainty of 2.34×10⁻¹⁸, and find it
consistent with the NIST–JILA campus value [Aeppli et al., PRL 137,
033201 (2026)] within the combined standard uncertainty. Together with
the NIST–JILA result, this provides the second, independent
determination of the Yb/Sr ratio by a different institute, as required
by criterion I.2.b for this pair of transitions.

Beyond the frequency ratio itself, the measurement addresses two
questions that matter for the redefinition and for future clock
networks. First, we show that the transfer infrastructure is no longer
the bottleneck at this level: the fiber link and the two frequency
combs contribute well below the clocks, and the dominant budget terms
are the per-segment statistics and the geodetic determination of the
gravitational potential difference. Second, we document the treatment
of the time-varying gravitational redshift with two independent tide
models (ETideLoad4.5 and a DE421+FES2022 combination): both degrade
the fit when applied as corrections and disagree in the sign of the
induced center shift, so we carry the term as an uncertainty item
rather than a correction. All numbers in the manuscript are generated
by a reproducible pipeline from a frozen, audited result layer; the
data and code are available at
https://github.com/hjiang555-a11y/gravitation-whsh under the Apache
License 2.0.

We believe this work is of broad interest to the PRL readership: it
completes, at intercity scale, the class of frequency-ratio
measurements on which the redefinition of the SI second depends, and
it demonstrates a working node pair of the wide-area optical-clock
network envisioned by the roadmap.

This manuscript has not been published previously and is not under
consideration elsewhere. [A companion full account with the complete
uncertainty budget, leveling details, and tidal-model comparison is in
preparation for Metrologia / will be provided as Supplemental
Material.]
