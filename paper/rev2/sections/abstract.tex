Redefining the International System of Units (SI) second requires
independent frequency-ratio measurements between different types of
optical clocks in various laboratories, with an overall measurement
uncertainty below \refThreshold{} and consistent results. However,
although numerous optical clocks have already reported self-evaluated
uncertainties below \selfEvalBound, validation of these self-assessments
through ratio measurements has not yet been completed. These
high-performance optical clocks are typically large in size and
structurally fragile, necessitating advanced network links for
comparison measurements and future applications. In this study, we
compared a ytterbium lattice clock in Wuhan with a strontium lattice
clock approximately $\siteKm$\,km away in Shanghai via a
phase-stabilised fibre link spanning $\fibreKm$\,km. Using data
collected over \nDays~consecutive days (approximately \nHours~hours), we
determined the Yb/Sr frequency ratio as
Yb/Sr~=~\Rheadline(\RheadlineUnc), with a Bayesian statistical
measurement uncertainty of \uStatShort$\times10^{-18}$ and a total
uncertainty of \uTotalShort$\times10^{-18}$. The central value differs
from the NIST--JILA result by \dVsNistE{} within the threshold range
required for redefining the SI second. To our knowledge, this is the
first inter-city, cross-species frequency-ratio measurement to meet
the uncertainty requirements outlined in the roadmap for redefining
the SI second.
