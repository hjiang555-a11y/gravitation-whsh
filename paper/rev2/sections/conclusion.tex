We have compared an ytterbium lattice clock in Wuhan and a strontium
lattice clock in Shanghai over a \fibreKm\,km fiber link and
determined the Yb/Sr frequency ratio with a total standard
uncertainty of \uTotalShort$\times10^{-18}$, the first intercity,
cross-species ratio measurement to meet the uncertainty requirement of
the roadmap for the redefinition of the SI second. Together with the
published same-campus and continental-network ratios, the three
independent determinations now agree at the few-$10^{-18}$ level. The
comparison shows that the transfer infrastructure is no longer the
bottleneck; what remains are the systematic evaluations of the clocks
and the geodetic treatment of the gravitational potential, including a
time-varying term that the available tide models cannot yet correct.
With these handled, the two sites become a working node pair of a
global optical-clock network built from fiber backbones joined by
satellite and free-space links.
