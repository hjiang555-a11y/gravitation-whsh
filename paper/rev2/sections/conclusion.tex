We have compared an ytterbium lattice clock in Wuhan and a strontium
lattice clock in Shanghai over a \fibreKm\,km fiber link and
determined the Yb/Sr frequency ratio with a total standard
uncertainty of \uTotalE$\times10^{-18}$. Together with the NIST--JILA
measurement, this provides, for the first time, two independent
determinations of the Yb/Sr ratio in agreement at the precision
required by the roadmap for the redefinition of the SI second,
satisfying criterion I.2.b of the roadmap for this pair of
transitions. The two sites now
form a working node pair of a distributed optical-clock network: with
more optical-clock nodes joined through terrestrial fiber backbones
and interconnected across continents by satellite and free-space
links, the comparison demonstrated here extends naturally into the
global network on which the redefinition, chronometric leveling of
the gravitational potential, and distributed searches for new physics
will depend.
