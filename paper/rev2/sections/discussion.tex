The value reported here agrees with the NIST--JILA measurement at the
level set by the redefinition roadmap
(Figure~\ref{fig:history}): relative to the NIST--JILA value
\refNistV(\refNistU)~\citep{aeppli2026nist} our central value differs
by $\dVsNistE$, well within the \refThreshold{} agreement threshold
of the roadmap's frequency-ratio
criterion~\citep{dimarcq2024roadmap, cctf2025taskforce}, and within
the combined standard uncertainty of the two measurements. The
European-network value
\refEuV(\refEuU)~\citep{pizzocaro2026european}, which differs from
ours by $\dVsEuE$, provides a supporting cross-check at a larger
uncertainty of \refEuE$\times10^{-17}$, above the redefinition
threshold.

The evolution of the Yb/Sr ratio also shows how comparison
measurements drive the field forward. Since
2015~\citep{nemitz2016ybsr}, independent ratios were measured with
increasing precision, yet the determinations disagreed well beyond
their quoted uncertainties---an early sign of common-mode errors in
the absolute frequency chain on which the earlier values
relied~\citep{beloy2021bacon}. The successive comparisons exposed
those errors and guided their elimination
(Figure~\ref{fig:history}).

Changes in the Earth's gravitational potential enter the comparison
directly. The static difference between the two clock transitions is
fixed by first-order spirit leveling and applied as a correction,
although it rests on a single leveling campaign whose available
GNSS-plus-geoid-model calibration is too imprecise (Methods) to act
as an effective cross-check; closing this gap will require
transportable optical clocks with higher frequency reproducibility,
rather than geodetic techniques alone. Of the time-varying
contributions, the tidal term is already appreciable at our precision
and is therefore carried as a data-driven uncertainty item rather
than corrected (Section~\ref{sec:results-budget}), while co-seismic
steps from the regional seismicity during the campaign fall well
below the reported precision.

The tidal correlation is nevertheless real. Within each segment, the
demeaned beat waveform tracks the tidal template at a level that both
models reproduce (Figure~\ref{fig:tidal-response}): for the \nSeg~
segments entering the ratio combination, the sign of the
within-segment correlation is negative in \tidalNegSeg~out of
\nSeg~segments for either model, and the precision-weighted response
amplitude is $\tidalAmpOldE$ for the ETideLoad4.5 template and
$\tidalAmpNewE$ for the DE421+FES2022 template. The correlation is
statistically significant, but no template corresponds to it: the two
amplitudes differ from each other and both fall short of the full
expected response of $-1$, and the two models also disagree in the
sign of the center shift they produce (Table~\ref{tab:tidal}). Because
the observed coupling is smaller than the templates predict, the true
tidal impact is likely no larger than the carried uncertainty item.
The templates are therefore incomplete at the $10^{-18}$ level, and
the model disagreement can be refined, or removed, by more comparison
data from an expanding optical-clock network.

The effect grows with the scale of the comparison: on
longer, continental- and intercontinental-scale baselines the
time-varying redshift becomes one of the dominant systematic
terms~\citep{denker2018geodetic}. That same sensitivity turns the
network into an instrument for the Earth: clock comparisons over
large baselines directly sense the time-variable gravitational
potential, providing an independent means of testing and calibrating
tide and loading models at the $10^{-18}$ level. Conversely, tidal
knowledge gained in this way feeds back into the comparisons
themselves: with data-consistent models, the time-varying term can be
corrected rather than carried, extending the accuracy reach of
large-scale clock comparisons. A fully self-consistent treatment of
the time-dependent potential would require a dedicated geodetic
campaign with in-situ gravimetry and site-specific loading models and
is left to future work. The provenance of the supplied tide series
and the exact accounting of the full sample totals remain external
items pending confirmation.

Looking ahead, the result is a milestone on the necessary path
toward a wide-area optical-clock network: the building blocks are now
in place---terrestrial fiber links of the
kind used here~\citep{schioppo2022cavities, chen2026dissemination}
carry comparisons over thousands of kilometers within continents,
while free-space and satellite-based links are closing the remaining
gaps~\citep{pizzocaro2021vlbi, shen2021satellite,
shen2022freespace, caldwell2023geo}. The natural next step is a
hybrid architecture in which optical-clock nodes on continental fiber
backbones are interconnected across oceans by satellite or free-space
optical links, so that any pair of clocks on Earth can be compared at
the level demonstrated here. Realizing it will put the same demands
on geodetic infrastructure that this comparison
faced~\citep{grotti2018geodesy, mcgrew2018geodesy}.
