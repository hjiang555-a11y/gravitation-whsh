\subsection{Network and observation campaign}
\label{sec:results-network}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figs/fig1_network.png}
  \caption{The two-clock network. An IAPMST ytterbium clock in Wuhan and
  a USTC strontium clock in Shanghai are connected by a phase-stabilized
  \qty{1550}{nm} fiber link. Relay stations along the route carry
  erbium-doped fiber amplifiers and fiber-noise-cancellation loops.}
  \label{fig:network}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:network} shows the two-clock network. A ytterbium (Yb)
lattice clock at IAPMST~\citep{zhu2026yb} in Wuhan and a strontium (Sr)
lattice clock at USTC~\citep{jia2026sr} in Shanghai are
separated by $\siteKm$\,km and compared through a $\fibreKm$\,km
phase-stabilized fiber link on a commercial telecom network used
non-exclusively, with the carrier transmitted from Shanghai to Wuhan
(Methods). Both combs are self-referenced and phase-locked to their
local clock, so the two clocks are the system's only independent
frequency sources; the recorded quantity is the out-of-loop beat
between the received carrier and the local Yb-referenced comb,
sampled every second and normalized to the carrier frequency
$f_{1550}$, with the fiber loop-back monitored out of loop
throughout; all optical phase-locked loops in the chain are likewise
monitored to evaluate the status of the experiment. The geopotential
difference between the two clocks was
measured by spirit leveling to be
$\dW = \dWVal \pm \dWUnc~\mathrm{m^{2}\,s^{-2}}$ (Methods).

\subsection{Clock-ratio determination}
\label{sec:results-ratio}

Over two months (\nDays~days) the two clocks and their transfer chains
ran synchronously for a total of \nHours~effective hours, recorded as
\nSeg~jump-free segments in three measurement stages defined by the
experiment team (late June to early July, early August, and late
August); several of the segment boundaries
correspond to deliberate clock shutdowns and restarts, so the
reproducibility of the ratio rests on tested restart behavior rather
than on continuous operation. The segment estimates show no
systematic drift over the campaign, and the network tolerates
interruption, with each retained window self-consistently
characterized. The results are presented in
Figure~\ref{fig:ratio}.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figs/fig2_ratio.png}
  \caption{Frequency-ratio measurement results. (a) Fractional
  instability of a representative segment (Yb/Sr, segment 8); the
  gray markers are the out-of-loop instabilities of the two frequency
  combs (SH, WH) and of the fiber link. (b) Per-segment ratio
  deviations from the final reported value. (c) The same deviations
  by campaign date.}
  \label{fig:ratio}
\end{figure}

We determine the Yb/Sr frequency ratio from the \nSeg~segments. Each
segment yields a ratio estimate with its own statistical uncertainty
(Methods). Following recent ratio
comparisons~\citep{pizzocaro2026european, aeppli2026nist}, we combine
the segments with a random-effect model that admits a common excess
scatter~\citep{birge1932errors, mandel1970interlaboratory}, and
evaluate three estimators (Table~\ref{tab:estimators}). The three
agree at the $10^{-18}$ level; their center values spread by
$\dEstSpread$ in fractional units. We adopt the Bayesian random-effect
estimate, which carries the largest standard uncertainty of the
three, as the primary value,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Yb/Sr} = \Rheadline,
\end{equation}
with statistical uncertainty $\uStatE\times10^{-18}$.

\subsection{Uncertainty budget}
\label{sec:results-budget}

Beyond the statistical uncertainty of the ratio combination, the
comparison carries several systematic contributions: the published
evaluations of the two clocks, the geodetic determination of the
gravitational-potential difference between the sites, and the
residuals of the transfer subsystems. We discuss each in turn and
combine them into the total standard uncertainty
(Table~\ref{tab:budget}).

The Sr clock contributes $\uSrE\times10^{-18}$ from its published
systematic evaluation~\citep{jia2026sr}, and the Yb clock
$\uYbE\times10^{-18}$~\citep{zhu2026yb}. The static
gravitational-potential term is the leveling uncertainty
$\dWUnc~\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$ converted to fractional frequency as
$u(\dW)/c^2$, giving $\uStaticE\times10^{-18}$; this is the same
leveled geopotential difference already applied as the static
correction (Section~\ref{sec:results-network}). The frequency
conversion stages each contribute below $\uSubsystemBound$ from the
digitization error of the direct-digital-synthesis
frequency-reference settings, carried as $\uLinkE\times10^{-18}$ and
$\uCombE\times10^{-18}$ upper bounds in the budget.

The time-varying gravitational redshift is more involved. Solid Earth
and ocean tides, together with atmospheric, hydrological and
non-tidal ocean loading, move the local potential by amounts reaching
the $10^{-18}$ level over the campaign~\citep{agnew2015tides,
voigt2016timevariable, denker2018geodetic}. Correcting the beat record
with a tide template from the public software
ETideLoad4.5~\citep{zhang2026etideload}, which implements the IERS
Conventions (2010)~\citep{iers2010conventions}, degrades rather than
improves the fit, raising the reduced chi-square of the combination
from \tidalChiRaw~to \tidalChiEmp, and a second, independent template
built from the DE421 ephemerides~\citep{folkner2009de421} and the
FES2022 ocean-tide model~\citep{lyard2021fes2014} does the same
($\chi^2_{\rm red} = \tidalChiAlt$) while moving the center in the
opposite direction, by $-\tidalShiftEmpE\times10^{-18}$ versus
$+\tidalShiftAltE\times10^{-18}$ (Methods). The sign disagreement
between the two models confirms that the templates are incomplete at
this level, so the time-varying term is carried as an uncertainty
rather than a correction, at the larger of the two model shifts,
$\uTidalE\times10^{-18}$ (Methods).

Combining the statistical and systematic components in quadrature
gives the total standard uncertainty
$\uTotalE\times10^{-18}$; the reported ratio is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Yb/Sr} = \Rheadline(\RheadlineUnc),
\end{equation}
where the parenthesized digits are the total standard uncertainty in
the last two decimal places of the value.
